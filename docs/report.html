Selected: a local anatomical annotation error.

On the same frozen CT audit, Terra/max and Sol/xhigh both completed normally but missed a small kidney-mask extension. It is a concrete, reviewable difficulty lead with a one-file deliverable.

These are development-selected pilot observations, one per model configuration. They do not establish a failure rate or complete TB3 qualification. The clean submission and its evaluation status are maintained separately.

01 / THE TASK

A plausible mask. A local mistake.

Review eleven anatomical labels in one CT scan and its DICOM segmentation (the medical-imaging file format). Report incorrect labels and a point locating each error. The answer is a small JSON file.

What the ground truth does—and does not—establish

The controlled edit adds 63 voxels: 43 were background and 20 were iliopsoas in the source labels. Forty-four changed voxel centers are more than 3 mm from the original kidney mask centers. Independent re-scoring agrees with both pilot grades.

The source contours are not specialist-certified clinical truth. A newly alleged source-label defect needs review before a zero reward can be called a model failure. This limitation mattered in other patients and led to explicit exclusions and a held case.

02 / RESEARCH JOURNEY

Four milestones, with useful dead ends.

These milestones summarize the reasoning, rather than inventing a straight chronological path. The DICOM work continued alongside geometry and Xiangqi branches. Original round IDs retain the actual history.

M1 · SearchStart broad; test whether the difficulty survives extraction

The workshop inspected published task-level failures and built a 100-card bank spanning science, geometry, CAD, compilers, data and media. Its first screen advanced two candidates, held fifty and rejected forty-eight; a second search added twelve records. Those counts describe source screening, not model trials.

Six compact scientific extractions in BR-001 all passed Terra/high. Four BR-002 tasks all passed both Sol/max and Astra/max. The small tasks often removed complications responsible for the original source failure. Published difficulty did not transfer automatically.

Decision: retire solved snapshots and preserve the counterevidence instead of repeatedly making them harder.

M2 · AuditCheck whether the task gives away its own solution

A package audit covered twenty unique tasks and twenty-four completed passes before BR-005. Five supplied a central recipe or reference, seven substantially directed the solution, and eight retained the main reasoning problem. These are qualitative assessments, not twenty new trials.

Five corrected packages were retested in BR-005: six model runs produced five passes and one reviewed Clipper failure. Later, Sol/xhigh passed BR-007's strengthened 144-case Clipper condition. The earlier Terra miss remained valid; the stronger-model difficulty target was not met.

Lesson: passing oracle/nop controls says the grader distinguishes answers; it does not say discovering the answer is difficult. The audit was useful, but it came later than it should have.

BR-003 used work-history conversations to construct PDF, routing and DICOM prototypes. All four passed Terra/high. BR-004 revisited anatomical QA with localized alterations and spatial evidence, rather than label presence alone.

Four Sol/xhigh attempts completed normally: three reviewed controlled-defect misses and one source hold. Cases 32 and 83 retained the earlier Terra task bytes; cases 46 and 61 used explicit regional exclusions and must be interpreted separately.

03 / WORKING WITH AGENTS

Agents accelerated the loop. Evidence governed the decisions.

Human direction

Human direction covered the assignment, the non-security and short-horizon scope, work-history context, challenges to easy results and misleading scaffolding, and the choice of follow-up experiments.

Agent-assisted work

Agents searched sources, built task packages and verifiers, executed authorized trials, inspected traces, proposed interpretations and maintained records. This report was also prepared with agent assistance.

The key control was to keep three judgments separate: did the run complete correctly, did the artifact pass, and does that outcome support the difficulty hypothesis?

How the research kept a failure claim reviewable
  1. Declare the question. Specify the task, intended unknown, acceptance rule, controls and stop condition.
  2. Freeze the inputs. Retain task bytes, source provenance, harness settings and attempt identities.
  3. Validate the verifier. Run known-correct, empty-answer and targeted wrong-answer controls. Clean cases may correctly accept an empty answer.
  4. Run and inspect. Separate timeouts and infrastructure errors from completed artifacts; replay the submitted answer and inspect concrete mismatches.
  5. Keep the uncomfortable result. Retire passes, hold disputed ground truth, preserve defects and never rewrite an old freeze to fit a new explanation.

This describes a documented, human-directed workflow. It does not claim unaided agent discovery or an autonomous research system. Trace checks constrain observed assistance; they cannot prove provider-side model identity or eliminate every possible information channel.

05 / LESSONS & DISCUSSION

What this investigation changed.

What worked

  • Use real work to find a meaningful decision with a small deliverable.
  • Preserve same-task controls and independently replay submitted artifacts.
  • Review source uncertainty before promoting a failure.
  • Keep solved tasks and failed hypotheses visible.

What did not

  • Assume a miniature inherits the source benchmark's difficulty.
  • Supply a central recipe and interpret success as discovery.
  • Use a long batch timeout as evidence of reasoning failure.
  • Equate a stronger verifier with a harder task for the model.
Interview discussion: what would you change first?

Move the solver-visible scaffolding audit before the first model run. Write down the exact inference the task leaves unknown, then inspect helpers, examples and bundled references for shortcuts. The workshop identified this problem but did not consistently prevent it in later authoring.

Audit findings and process failure

Interview discussion: why select case 32 instead of claiming broad model weakness?

It combines a small deliverable, a controlled local error, two same-task reviewed misses and lower observed resources than the other reviewed Sol misses. The evidence is confined to a development-selected patient and configurations. It cannot establish a general weakness in medical reasoning or a reliable failure probability.

Interview discussion: what is the hardest verification tradeoff?

A fixed exact-set grader makes evaluation fast and repeatable, but cannot automatically adjudicate a new legitimate finding in the original source labels. Case 46 demonstrated the problem. Source review and explicit holds are part of the validity judgment; a deterministic score alone is insufficient.

Interview discussion: how much did the agents contribute?

Substantially: research, code, trial operation, trace inspection, analysis and presentation were agent-assisted. The interview can focus on the human direction and decisions, the artifacts agents produced, and the evidence used to accept or reject their conclusions. This report does not present generated code or prose as exclusively human-authored.

Possible follow-up work

These are unexecuted research questions, not an active backlog or promised results.

  1. Remove rendering overhead. Supply a standard viewer while preserving the patient, error and scope. Test whether the miss survives clearer views and less rendering-code work.
  2. Resolve source-label ambiguity. Obtain appropriate adjudication before treating disputed anatomy as grader truth or expanding to more patients.
  3. Test stability separately from selection. Evaluate a newly declared repeatability protocol. A narrower kidney-only task is another contrast and should not be combined with a viewer change if the aim is to understand either effect.
