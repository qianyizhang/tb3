# Read change without overstating what the images establish

**Longitudinal MRI · BR-037 · exploratory, clinically unadjudicated**

The useful capability is linking measurements, image-cited observations and
uncertainty across visits. A mechanically complete report is not a clinical pass.

## See the task

The [protocol](../../../docs/research-rounds/BR-037-longitudinal-reading.md)
uses three public I-SPY2 patients. Four visits were retained for source review;
only the first two were exposed to the agents. Future images, source measurements,
pathology outcomes and diagnosis-bearing private tags were withheld.

## Why this work matters

Diameter, enhancement-derived volume and pathology describe different things.
They can disagree within a patient. A useful longitudinal reader must identify
its measurement method and evidence, acknowledge missing measurements, and avoid
turning reduced enhancement into a claim of pathological clearance.

## What the agent received and returned

Each packet contained full reconstructed examinations, native geometry, sequence
names and relative timing. Deliverables included an assessment, report, analysis
code and figures. One case had a second independent attempt with an added,
unverified claim that the abnormality had disappeared.

## What worked, and what it cost

The [retained review](../../../docs/research-rounds/BR-037-results.md) records four
normally completed Terra/high attempts that passed the mechanical contract.
They localized the dominant finding and avoided declaring pathological clearance.
Two neutral runs declined follow-up diameter measurement. The cue pair produced
different shrinkage estimates using different methods. This leaves measurement
reliability unresolved; one cue pair cannot establish a general prompt effect.

Source selection was deliberately stratified. The cases support qualitative
analysis, not prevalence, population accuracy or a composite clinical pass rate.
No Sol comparison is included. The earlier BR-018 scaffold remains historical
context; it is not evidence of a completed experiment.
